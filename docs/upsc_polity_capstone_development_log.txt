6. FACT VERIFICATION
============================================================

FactVerifier outputs:
SUPPORTED / CONTRADICTED / INSUFFICIENT.

Principles:
- Only supplied evidence
- No outside knowledge
- Complete evidence required for SUPPORTED
- Explicit conflict required for CONTRADICTED
- INSUFFICIENT when evidence is incomplete

Negative claim handling was added using:

Purpose:
Handle claims such as:

============================================================
8. Q58 DETAILED FINDING
============================================================

Statement I contains two separate factual components:

1. President decides relevant parliamentary disqualification questions and the
   decision is final.
2. President acts in accordance with Council of Ministers' advice.

Initial ClaimRetriever results:
- Constitution p.77: Article 103
- Constitution p.378: Tenth Schedule
- Constitution p.117: Article 192

The missing Council of Ministers evidence was NOT a corpus limitation.

A deterministic grep of chunks.jsonl found Article 74 on Constitution p.65:
Council of Ministers aids and advises the President, and the President acts in
accordance with such advice.

Therefore the problem is:

Original compound claim
-> single retrieval query
-> one required constitutional provision is missed

This is a multi-hop / compound-claim retrieval problem.

Important:
Do not force the verifier to mark the claim SUPPORTED merely because the
official PYQ answer is known.

============================================================
9. CURRENT ARCHITECTURAL LESSON
============================================================

Current verification:
Claim
-> independent retrieval
-> FactVerifier

Observed limitation:
A compound claim may require multiple constitutional provisions.

Planned architecture:
Compound claim
-> decomposition into atomic subclaims
-> independent retrieval for each subclaim
-> verification of each subclaim
-> aggregate result

Example for Q58:
Subclaim 1:
Relevant parliamentary disqualification questions are decided by the President
and the decision is final.
Evidence: Article 103, p.77.

Subclaim 2:
The President acts in accordance with the advice of the Council of Ministers.
Evidence: Article 74, p.65.

Aggregation:
- all supported -> SUPPORTED
- any explicit contradiction -> CONTRADICTED
- otherwise -> INSUFFICIENT

10. IMPORTANT FAILURE-ANALYSIS PRINCIPLES
============================================================

Do not:
- weaken verification just to increase the score
- use official answers to influence verification
- treat missing evidence as contradiction
- treat failure to retrieve a term as proof of absence
- invent evidence
- silently add outside sources

Do:
- preserve baseline results
- separate retrieval, verification, reasoning and mapping failures
- use deterministic parsing when reproducibility matters
- use independent retrieval for verification
- record every modification and test

14. FIX PASS: 2026-09-27
============================================================

DATE:
2026-09-27

STEP:
Apply every defect found by the System C ablation and the label audit to
the real pipeline, before writing any deliverable.

WHAT WE DID:
Ran the six-arm single-variable ablation, audited the gold labels, then
fixed what the measurements exposed rather than what looked wrong.

WHAT WENT WRONG / OBSERVATION:

1. Retrieval depth. Claim verification queried top_k=3 while the
   retriever was selected on a Recall@5 benchmark. One integer. It was
   the largest single accuracy change in the project and it beat both
   interventions that were actually designed.

2. Gold label Q58 is wrong. Stored label A ("I only"); both statements
   are false against the corpus, so the answer is D. Five of six arms
   said D. The label error inverted the gradient: it made a FALSE
   SUPPORTED look like the project's best result.
   Statement II (no mention of the phrase "political party") is false:
   an exhaustive scan of all 1149 chunks finds the phrase 12 times
   across pages 65, 104, 251, 376, 377, 378, 379. Pages 65 and 104
   never appeared in any top-k retrieval, which is why the scan had to
   be exhaustive rather than ranked.

3. Answer-key verifier leaked the answer. The prompt contained a
   DECLARED ANSWER block plus a rule telling the model not to treat it
   as evidence. Independence was conditional on instruction-following.

4. The absence checker used BM25 top-5 to decide a claim that
   quantifies over the whole corpus. On this data it was correct by
   luck.

5. The orchestration node built FactVerifier() with no corpus, which
   silently disabled the absence checker, so the orchestrated pipeline
   was weaker than the thing being measured.

6. The evaluation harness carried a second copy of the option-mapping
   logic, and the copy held the bugs: the negated flag was computed then
   discarded, and the option text "None" parsed as neither a statement
   set nor a count, so it could never match.

7. The harness scored abstention as an incorrect answer, so every
   abstention the verifier added read as a regression.

8. The ablation harness hard-coded the vanilla RAG baseline at the
   stored-label figure (38.46%) while reporting every arm under stored
   AND audited labels. Under audited labels the baseline is 46.15%,
   because vanilla RAG answered D on Q58.

DECISION:

Ship bound claims even though C3d ties C3 on accuracy and beats it on
wrong answers. Claim soundness is measured over 37 claims and needs no
gold label; the one-question accuracy difference is measured over 13
questions against a label set with a confirmed error. The tie is
recorded in both modules rather than buried, and a reader who reverses
the call on this evidence is not making a mistake.

Do not claim the accuracy lead. Against the corrected baseline the best
arm leads by one question, which at 7.7 points per question is noise.
The claim that survives is the error rate: 7 of 13 wrong for vanilla RAG
against 1 of 13, bought by answering 8 of 13 instead of all 13.

Stop adding rules to the fact verifier. Appending rules 25-26 fixed
Q63 II and also flipped Q58 I on identical evidence at temperature 0,
with no rule mentioning anything in it. At 26 rules a rule is not an
isolated control. The next verifier change should decompose the prompt.

WHAT WE DID:

WHAT WENT WRONG / OBSERVATION:

1. The install failure was misdiagnosed for weeks. huggingface_hub
   reported LocalEntryNotFoundError "check your connection", so it was
   recorded as a network block. It was not. urllib reached huggingface.co
   with status 200 while httpx failed on the same URL, which isolates it:
   the connection is TLS-intercepted by a corporate proxy, the private
   root is in the Windows certificate store (which urllib uses) and
   absent from certifi (which httpx pins). Four lines fix it -
   truststore.inject_into_ssl(), the same mechanism as pip's
   --use-feature=truststore.

   Cost of the misdiagnosis: the main ablation table ran on a stdlib
   BM25 index, which is what produced the retracted conclusions below.
   A library's error classification is a hypothesis, not evidence.

2. FactVerifier never set temperature. The Responses API defaults to 1.0,
   so the verifier was a stochastic classifier. Meanwhile
   rag/vanilla_rag.py DID pass temperature=0. So every System B number
   ever reported was a single draw measured against a deterministic
   reference, and the whole difference was attributed to verification.

   Five call sites were unpinned: fact_verifier, claim_extractor,
   answer_key_verifier, mcq_quality_auditor, mcq_generator. All pinned.

   Found by noticing the ablation harness returned SUPPORTED 3/3 on
   Q58 item I while the shipped pipeline returned CONTRADICTED,
   CONTRADICTED, INSUFFICIENT on what should have been the same input.

3. Temperature 0 is not determinism. verdict_stability.py re-verified all
   37 claims 5x in both conditions:

     unpinned  28/37 stable (75.68%), 9 unstable, 2 emitting all three
               verdicts across 5 identical calls
     pinned    35/37 stable (94.59%), 2 unstable, 1 still emitting all
               three verdicts

   Recombining the independent per-claim draws into 5 independent
   pipeline runs gives a metric RANGE rather than a point estimate:

     unpinned  acc 23.08-38.46%  (spread 15.38 pts)
     pinned    acc 23.08-38.46%  (spread 15.38 pts)

   Pinning cut the number of unstable claims by 78% and moved the
   accuracy spread by zero, because the two survivors (Q58, Q63) are
   exactly the ones that swing a predicted option.

   Three harness runs disagreed with each other on the unstable count
   (27, 29, 28 of 37 unpinned), so at REPEATS=5 the measured instability
   is a LOWER BOUND, not an estimate.

4. Q58 item I is a decision-boundary claim, and the boundary is made of
   nuisance factors. Holding the claim and its five evidence pages fixed,
   at temperature 0 throughout, a 2x2 over {shipped prompt, harness
   prompt} x {responses.parse, chat.completions}:

     shipped prompt + responses.parse    -> INSUFFICIENT x3
     shipped prompt + chat.completions   -> SUPPORTED x3
     harness prompt + responses.parse    -> SUPPORTED x3
     harness prompt + chat.completions   -> SUPPORTED x3

   The two prompts differ by THREE CHARACTERS OF WHITESPACE and are
   otherwise byte-identical, rules included. Only the conjunction of the
   shipped prompt and the responses.parse endpoint gives INSUFFICIENT.
   Both factors necessary, neither sufficient.

5. THREE CONCLUSIONS FROM ENTRY 14 ARE RETRACTED. They were artifacts of
   the BM25 index, and this is the direct cost of item 1.

   (a) "Retrieval depth 3->5 was the largest single accuracy change in
       the project." False. On the real retriever the sign depends on the
       label set - stored labels say depth helps (15.38 -> 30.77% acc),
       audited labels say it hurts (precision 75.00 -> 60.00%, error rate
       7.69 -> 15.38%). A change whose sign depends on the label set is
       not an effect. And the whole of it runs through one verdict:
       Q58 I CONTRADICTED at k=3 -> SUPPORTED at k=5, which is precisely
       the boundary claim from item 4. The "depth effect" was one unstable
       claim being resampled.

       What survives is the weaker, cheaper version: the retriever was
       validated at Recall@5 and queried at k=3, so a configuration was
       running at settings it was never validated at. Worth fixing on
       principle. Not worth an accuracy claim.

   (b) "Rules 25-26 fixed Q63 II." False on the real retriever: C2->C3
       and C1d->C3d both change NO verdict at all. Byte-identical metrics
       and verdict counts. The rules stay in the prompt as harmless, not
       as measured. Entry 14 also said the Q58 flip happened "at
       temperature 0" - see item 2; temperature was never set, so that
       was one sample from an unpinned sampler.

   (c) "C3d ties C3 on accuracy and beats it on wrong answers, so the arm
       I did not ship looks better." Gone. C3 and C3d are now identical
       on every end-to-end metric (38.46/60.00/23.08/15.38 audited) and
       differ only in claim soundness. The decision got easier for the
       reason it should have been easy all along.

6. THE WHOLE SIX-ARM TABLE IS INSIDE THE NOISE BAND. Every arm-to-arm
   difference is 7.69 points (one question) or zero. The measured spread
   from item 3 is 15.38 points. So none of the six arms can be
   distinguished end to end at n=13, and the table must be reported as
   six null results rather than as a set of effects.

   What DOES clear the band, because it has 37 observations instead of 13
   and needs no gold label at all:

     propositional rate    70.27% -> 100%   (binding, both depths)
     false SUPPORTED       7-8    -> 0      (binding, both depths)
     hallucinated claims   19     -> 1      (A vs B, judge, extractive)

   These are the signals the architecture argument now rests on.

7. Nothing enforced the order the evaluation modules run in, and they

DECISION / REASONING:

Report the six-arm table as null. It is tempting to keep the depth
finding - it was the cleanest story in the project - but a difference
smaller than the measured noise floor is not a finding, and the specific
verdict it runs through is the one claim that returns all three verdicts
for a living. Keeping it would be the same error as entry 14 item 2,
where a wrong label made a false SUPPORTED look like the best result:
optimising against a signal that is not measuring what it appears to.

Move the architecture argument to claim-level metrics. 37 observations,
no gold-label dependency, and the effects are large (70.27% -> 100%,
8 -> 0, 19 -> 1) rather than one-question. This is also the honest
answer to "why ship bound claims" now that the accuracy columns are
indistinguishable.

Keep the headline. Vanilla RAG states a false answer on 7 of 13
(audited) or 8 of 13 (stored); the verified system on 0 of 13 under both.
That is roughly six questions, retriever-matched, far outside a
two-question band. It is the one end-to-end result worth defending, and
it is a coverage trade, not an accuracy win - vanilla RAG beats every
verified arm on accuracy under both label sets, which the write-up now
states plainly instead of arranging around.

Measure the instrument before the system. The verifier's noise floor
should have been the first thing measured, not the last. Every
single-variable ablation run before it was an attempt to resolve
differences smaller than the measurement error, which is why two of them
produced confident conclusions that a retriever swap deleted.

Prefer structural fixes to discipline. The ordering hazard in item 7 was
"just remember to run them in order" twice, and it failed twice. run_all
--check makes it checkable in a second, and it caught a stale output the
first time it ran.

The five retrieval benchmarks in EVALUATION.md section 1 (75.00 / 81.25 /
87.50 / 87.50 / 93.75 Recall@5) were NOT re-run in this pass. They are
deterministic given the corpus, so they should reproduce, but that is
unverified and the document says so rather than implying otherwise.

REPEATS=5 in verdict_stability.py is too few. Three runs of the harness
disagreed on the unstable-claim count, so the 9-and-2 figures are lower
bounds. 20 repeats would give a usable interval, and the claims and
evidence are already stored, so it is pure resampling.

Nothing is committed. All of this sits uncommitted on main.
